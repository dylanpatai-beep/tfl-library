## What it is

A small, fast stance switch that turns your lead leg into a power weapon. Done right, the switch and the kick are one motion — the opponent reads it as a twitch, not a telegraph.

## Setup

- From orthodox: small scissor hop — lead foot back, rear foot forward — landing already turned.
- The hop is horizontal, not vertical. Height telegraphs; sliding hides.
- Hands stay in the pocket during the switch. The classic tell is a dipping lead hand.

## The details that make it work

1. Kick on the landing, not after it. The hop loads the hip; hesitation dumps the power.
2. Turn the standing foot fully — the heel points at the target on impact.
3. Swing the same-side arm down and behind for rotation; the other hand guards.

## Common mistakes

- Big jumping switch that announces the kick a beat early.
- Kicking with the leg instead of the hip turn — it looks fast and lands soft.
- No plan for the catch. Pair it with the teep or follow behind punches so it isn't naked.
