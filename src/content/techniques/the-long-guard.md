## What it is

Instead of shelling up, the lead arm extends toward the opponent's face or collarbone — a live frame. It kills their jab before it starts, measures your own range, and keeps your rear side loaded.

## Setup

- Lead hand extends, palm or forearm to their face/collarbone. Not a push — a post.
- Rear hand stays glued to your head. The long guard invites the overhand; the rear hand is why it doesn't land.
- Elbow of the extended arm stays slightly bent. A locked arm gets pulled.

## The details that make it work

1. The frame targets the face, not the chest. On the face it disrupts vision and posture; on the chest it's just a reach.
2. Keep the lead shoulder raised — it covers the hook side your extended arm gives up.
3. Walk forward behind it. The long guard is a pressure tool, not a retreat.

## Common mistakes

- Reaching with the frame instead of placing it — you get parried and countered.
- Letting the rear hand drift while admiring the frame.
- Staying static. The frame is a doorway: elbow over the top, clinch entry, or teep as they pull out.
